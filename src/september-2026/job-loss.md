## The Weight of the Satchel
As Craig walked down the street, satchel in hand, contemplating all the things that had led up to that moment. His pace slow and steady as he recollected his achievements throughout the years which culminated in a major award from the Modern Language Association and a tenured position at Stanford University. Years studying and searching for evidence that Shakespeare had actually been a woman; combing through prose for words or phrases only used by women of the time. But this had meant very little when the Tenure Committee had been presented with some so-called ‘evidence’ of misconduct; when the president of the university, who he’s worked alongside for many years, told him that his career at Stanford was over.

Despite the sounds of the birds tweeting and the beautiful array of colors in the autumn leaves that ran like a rainbow down the street, Craig continued to plod on. The leaves crunched beneath his feet and a slow breeze blew, which only made him cling onto his satchel all the more. Eventually he reached his front door, turned the key and let the latch lock behind him. In one elegant movement he pulled the laptop and his dismissal notice out of the satchel, threw the satchel onto the sideboard and marched to his desk.

His eyes parsed over the letter again and again looking for the mistake, the error; it had to be in here somewhere. They’d dismissed him on the grounds that he had plagiarised and misquoted on multiple occasions in his work but that could not have been possible. Each paper had been peer reviewed by three other professors, had passed multiple plagiarism checkers and had been read by numerous people in the journal publications of his work. Craig didn’t sink much worth in the journal readers; anyone could simply take his word as gospel without a second thought but it was another layer of protection he had given himself.

Something else seemed wrong. He had barely left the campus before he had heard people congratulating Enzo Quentin on his new tenured position in Literature; the exact position that he himself had just been relieved from. He had read much of Quentins’s work, in most of it his analysis was obvious and his prose was weak at best; it was an example of quantity over quality, Craig thought. However, that thought would not detract him from proving his innocence. He started at the top of the list of accusations, the misquoted texts.

‘Paper 240, Section C, published in The Kenyon Review; misquoted _‘The lady protest too much, methinks’_, should have been _‘The lady doth protest too much, methinks’_’. He pulled up his laptop, and scrolled through his submissions. Finally he found the correct one. He paged through to find the section and there it stared him in his face. The quote they had stated to be incorrect was correct in its entirety. How could the university have made such a rookie error when reviewing the evidence? It didn’t make any sense. He fact checked a few more and sure enough all his submissions were correct. The only logical conclusion was that it had been changed pre-publication.

He compiled lists of everyone who worked at the journals at his publication times; searching for patterns in the names but there were too many of them to count; years of analytical study had prepared him for such a task unconsciously. Tackling the list like one of his research projects, he started finding links and means to classify all the individuals; each one giving him more information and leading him to the correct names. When he grouped the list by name and counted how many times the name occurred, it hit him. He merely paused for a moment, hand on laptop, eyes on the screen, his skills had yet again paid off.
Gently reaching for the bottle of whiskey in the bookshelf behind him, he poured himself a glass, the liquid sloshing uncontrollably, no ice to help break its fall. The bottle went down with a harder thunk than expected followed moments after by downing the glass in one. The name at the top had worked at every literary journal he had submitted to and had published in. A name that was all too familiar recently, Enzo Quentin.
