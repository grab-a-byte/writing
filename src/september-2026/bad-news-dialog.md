# Bad New Dialog
_Quite an easy to follow one for this week, make a story that is based on breaking some bad news and make it a dialog. The dialog part was the hardest for me as I'm used to writing about what's seen and happens as opposed to actual spoken words. It was a nice challenge._

## Between the Canvases

Detective Ted Johnson had spent most of the morning conversing with all the other members of the travelling circus act and finding out whatever he could. Some had suspected murder, others had simply deemed the whole thing an accident. Either way, the ringmaster lay dead among the canvases and there was only a single someone left to tell. A young boy, who couldn't have been older than seven, cycled around on his unicycle seemingly blissfully unaware as to what was going on around him. Ted looked at the boy and remembered when he’d lost his own father and the impact it had had on him as a young child. It was this that drove his steps forwards before taking a deep breath and releasing out his first words.

“Hello. You do well on that unicycle lad.” The boy beamed at the compliment while Ted just thought about how he’d been taught about the shit sandwich, raising them up first before delivering the bad news and trying to find something to salvage it at the end. “You been doing it long?” he questioned, feeling that guilt well up in his stomach.

“About 3 years. I learned it from my dad and just kept at it. I can turn, jump and even stay still on it these days!”. He couldn’t break the little boys heart just yet.

“Why don’t we go for a walk and you can show me all the acts? I’ve been asked to look around and feel like I haven’t seen half the place.”

“Ok, follow me!”.

The boy seemed ecstatic at the thought of being able to do something around the circus and he started rambling on. Telling stories left right and centre about the acts and all the places they’d been. It took a while before he decided it was time to tell the lad about what had happened.

“Listen lad, there’s something I need to tell you before we carry on our way. I’m afraid that your dad’s not going to be coming back.”
The boy never even stumbled, he just looked at Ted and responded with an innocent reply.

“I know, he left a while ago but he said he’ll be back when I’m 16.”

“I’m afraid that may not be happening anymore. I’m afraid that he may not be coming back at all, lad.” Something stuck with Ted though; the boy was travelling with the circus so why would his dad, the ringmaster, only be getting him when he’s 16.

“Nope.” The boy replied. “Dads gonna come get me when I’m 16. Told me to build some skills while I was here”.

Ted may have been taught some things about interrogation and had tried to apply the same techniques to letting the boy down and it didn’t seem to be going rather well. Not only that, the boy’s responses led him to believe he was very sheltered. There wasn’t going to be an easy way around this was there?

“Lad, your dad is dead. They found him in his tent not moving. I’m afraid that he’s not going to be able to come back.” Now he’d done it. It was required and necessary but it still felt cruel. He felt the pit in his stomach grow a thousand times larger. It was the same way someone had broken the news to him and he hated it then and assumed the boy would hate it now. The seconds before the response felt like an eternity.

“That’s not possible,” the boy laughed. “I just received a letter from my dad yesterday, he said he was moving to the next city to make some money.”

“So the ringmaster isn’t your father?”

“Nope. Is the ringmaster dead?” Now the boy’s face showed sorrow for the first time. His eyes started to well up and his breath started to shallow.

Ted pulled a packet of tissues from his pocket and handed them to the boy. “I’m afraid so, lad. I’m sorry to break the news. I’m also sorry I thought he was your father, someone told me he was.” At this point the boy started bawling, tears streamed now and snots flew. All Ted could do was pat the boy on the back and tell him it was going to be okay. He walked the boy back to the rest of the performers who looked at Ted with dismay as he walked into the tent. A woman ran to the boy, hugged him and she whispered in a voice barely enough to be heard, “It’s going to be okay son”.

Ted now had a new job. Who had fed him the misinformation, he’d have to go over his notes. He now had a new suspect.
